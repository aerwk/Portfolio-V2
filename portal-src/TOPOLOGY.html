Web Topology

Twelve hostnames on one Cloudflare zone, reaching origins by two different routes. Three of them are ours and share a stylesheet; the rest are third-party applications that must not.

How a request reaches an origin

Every hostname resolves through the same Cloudflare zone and then splits. The split is what decides whether a page can be public, and therefore where it is allowed to be hosted.

Where the stylesheet comes from

All three of our surfaces render from one assets/style.css in one repository. They reach the browser by two different mechanisms, which is the part that used to let them drift.

The rule this map exists to make obvious: a surface gets our stylesheet only if we wrote the page. That is ericli, www and portal — three of thirteen entries. The rest render their own applications, and restyling them would mean forking software to change its CSS.

Things worth knowing before changing any of it

  • The portal has no monitor. Neither do git, uptime, n8n or ha, and neither tunnel connector is watched as a process. The portal container was found stopped for two days on 13 Aug and nothing had reported it.
  • Two tunnel connectors serve every gated host, round-robin. An origin reachable from only one of them fails about half of all requests, and only after the Access check — so an unauthenticated probe still looks healthy.
  • The portal's loopback bind must stay. Tunnel ingress resolves localhost:8080; removing that bind has taken the portal down before, instantly.
  • Vercel serves everything in the repo. There is no build step to filter it, so exclusion is the only mechanism — proven with a canary that returned 404 on both public hosts while a control file still returned 200.
  • Keep the GitHub repo public. While private, Vercel produced deployments in state BLOCKED that never built, and production quietly kept serving the previous version.
