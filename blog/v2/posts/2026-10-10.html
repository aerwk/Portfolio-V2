PC Against the Cluster, Real Power Numbers and a Flaky Camera

Homelab 2026-10-10 · Saturday · 7:03 PM 4 min read Eric Li

From about 00:22 to 04:28 I worked on the benchmark and power numbers, with a break for research. From about 16:45 to 19:03 I was at a house fixing a camera.

PC against the cluster

I wanted to know whether heavy jobs belong on the PC or on the cluster. I ran the same compute benchmark on a self-generated input on the 4 vCPU sandbox VM on pve02 and on the RTX 5080 in my PC. The VM managed about 5,500 operations a second. The GPU did roughly 840,000 to 895,000, about 160 times more. The full run on the PC took about 2 hours 20 minutes at 75 degrees, 92% fan and 100% GPU use, with no throttling.

That settled the sandbox VM. It was no use for benchmarking, so I stopped it, destroyed it with its disk, and checked that no ZFS remnants were left. I marked its runsheet destroyed. A sed command meant to change two lines in the index indented every line instead. I caught it straight away, restored the file from git and redid the two edits.

What the nodes and my desk draw

To compare fairly I needed wall power, not CPU package numbers. The smart plugs under the nodes were mislabelled in both Home Assistant (names like the wine fridge and the washer) and UniFi, left over from the recabling. I loaded each node in turn for about 11 minutes while sampling all four plugs, which told me which plug fed which node. Then I renamed the Home Assistant devices to pve01 to pve04 and the UniFi clients to match. UniFi sometimes acknowledges a write it doesn't keep, so I checked the names again a minute later and they held.

The measurements:

  • Each of the three small nodes idles around 11 to 24 W and reaches roughly 47 to 59 W at full load.
  • pve04 is a different machine and idles at about 43 W with nothing running but the Talos sandbox. That is double the others.
  • All four nodes idle at about 96 W together.
  • The PC idles at about 85 W and drew about 440 W under the benchmark. The whole desk is 287 to 300 W idle and about 640 W under load.
  • The 49 inch ultrawide is about 120 W by itself, roughly what the four nodes idle at together. The two 27 inch monitors are about 40 W each.

At an assumed 30 cents a kWh, the three small nodes cost about 140 dollars a year and pve04 alone about 113. So always-on apps belong on the cluster, because idle power dominates, and the PC is only worth using for short heavy jobs. The Talos sandbox was the obvious waste. I shut down all three of its VMs at about 03:26 and pve04 dropped from about 43 W to about 15 W. I updated the project row and the pve04 runsheet with restart instructions.

Monitor research

The ultrawide's 120 W and the desk space it takes made me look at a 34 inch OLED replacement. The new generation of panel fixes the text fringing of the older ones. The best pick so far is the ASUS ROG Strix XG34WCDMS at 1,399 dollars, which has a three-year burn-in warranty and draws about 34 W. The Alienware AW3426DW is the alternative, and it sold for 1,155 to 1,199 dollars in September. Several retailer sites blocked my lookups, so some prices are partial. I haven't bought anything.

A camera dropping off at a house

In the evening I went to a house where a solar security camera in the garage showed "Connection Failed" while reporting a full battery. Another camera outside was fine. The garage door controller I'd just fitted also worked, because tiny on/off messages cope with a weak link and video doesn't.

I first suspected distance, with a concrete slab between floors, and considered moving the base station to the garage. Before buying anything I looked at the router's settings. The 2.4 GHz roaming assistant was on with a cutoff of minus 70 dBm, so the router kicked any 2.4 GHz device whose signal fell below that. A battery camera at the edge of coverage hit it constantly. I changed one thing at a time. Disabling the roaming assistant, synced to all three nodes, got the camera reconnected and staying up. I also set the 2.4 GHz channel width to 20 MHz, which doesn't affect the controller. To rule out distance, I put the base station back in its old spot and the camera still worked. No new switch, port or node is needed.

I also did a read-only look at the rest of the router settings and wrote up some hardening items for the homeowner to decide on. I won't list them here.

Still open

  • The 5 GHz roaming assistant is still at the same cutoff. Only 2.4 GHz caused the dropout.
  • I was about to run the firmware update on the mesh nodes, and I haven't confirmed the settings survive it.
  • The garage camera needs a recheck tomorrow, since the original dropout happened overnight.
  • UniFi shows all four node plugs offline while Home Assistant reads live watts from them. I haven't looked into it. The air purifier's power sensor has also read 0 W since the 6th.
  • No decision on the monitor.
